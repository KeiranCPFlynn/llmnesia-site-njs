Readwise and LLMnesia sit on opposite sides of your information workflow. Readwise captures and resurfaces highlights from what you read: articles, books, PDFs, newsletters. LLMnesia is a free, local-first Chrome extension that indexes the AI conversations you have across ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini and 10+ platforms. Readwise captures your input; LLMnesia captures your AI output. They complement each other more than they compete.

## LLMnesia vs Readwise at a glance

| | Readwise | LLMnesia |
|---|---|---|
| Captures | Highlights from reading | AI conversations |
| Content type | Static documents and articles | Dynamic chat sessions |
| AI chat history search | No | Yes, full text |
| Platforms indexed | Reading sources | ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, and 10+ more |
| Jump-back to original chat | No | Direct link |
| Where your data lives | Cloud (your Readwise account) | Local-first, on your device |
| Price | Paid subscription | Free |

## What Readwise does

Readwise is a reading-retention tool. It syncs highlights from Kindle, Instapaper, articles, PDFs, and other sources, and resurfaces them through spaced repetition so you remember what you read. Readwise Reader is its companion read-later app, with full-text search across your saved articles and the ability to chat with your own highlights. Readwise's value is the reading pipeline: capturing what you encounter, helping you remember it, and building a searchable library of highlights over time.

For AI-adjacent research, some users import articles and papers into Reader before using them as context for AI conversations. The article stays in Readwise; the AI conversation about it is separate.

## What LLMnesia does differently

LLMnesia is not a reading tool. It is a conversation-retrieval tool. It indexes your AI sessions, the questions you asked, the answers you received, the code generated, the analysis produced, and makes them searchable. Where Readwise captures what you read, LLMnesia captures what you got from AI.

## The content-type mismatch

Readwise handles documents: static content from a URL or file that you highlight specific passages from. Good highlights are short, sourced, and hand-picked. AI conversations are sessions: dynamic, multi-turn exchanges whose value comes from the whole thread, how it evolved, which questions led to which answers, and the context around a specific response, not a single highlighted sentence. Forcing AI conversations into Readwise means deciding what to highlight at the moment of the chat, which reintroduces the discipline problem LLMnesia removes by capturing everything automatically. The broader distinction is covered in [AI knowledge base vs chat history](/blog/ai-knowledge-base-vs-chat-history).

## A concrete example

You save three papers to Readwise Reader, highlight the key passages, then spend an afternoon with Claude and Perplexity turning them into an argument. Weeks later you need both the source quote and the conclusion you reached with AI. Readwise has the highlights; only LLMnesia has the AI sessions. You search `methodology limitation sample size` and the Claude analysis comes back with a link to the original chat.

## Can you use both together?

Yes, and research-heavy users often should. A common pattern:

1. Encounter a relevant article or paper.
2. Save it to Readwise Reader for reading and highlighting.
3. Use an AI tool to summarise, analyse, or extend the research.
4. The highlights live in Readwise; the AI conversation lives in LLMnesia's index.
5. When revisiting a question, search both: what you read, and what AI helped you conclude.

For this workflow the two tools are additive, not overlapping.

## Choosing between them

**Use Readwise if:**
- Your main problem is reading retention and highlight capture.
- You read heavily from web articles, books, or PDFs.
- You want spaced-repetition review of what you have read.

**Use LLMnesia if:**
- Your main problem is finding past AI conversation output.
- You use AI tools heavily for research, writing, or development.
- You want cross-platform search across ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, and others.
