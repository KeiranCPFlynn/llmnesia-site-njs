TypingMind and LLMnesia both promise searchable AI conversation history, but they put your history in different places. TypingMind is a chat client you use instead of the official apps, storing and searching the conversations you have inside it. LLMnesia is a free, local-first Chrome extension that indexes and searches the conversations you already have on ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini and 10+ platforms. The deciding question is simple: do you want to move your chatting into a new tool, or keep using the official apps and add search on top?

## LLMnesia vs TypingMind at a glance

| | TypingMind | LLMnesia |
|---|---|---|
| What it is | A chat client you use instead of the official apps | A search layer over the official apps you already use |
| Whose history it holds | Conversations you have inside TypingMind | Your existing ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, and 10+ platform chats |
| Sees your native web-app chats | No | Yes |
| Model access | Your own API keys | You use the platforms as normal; no keys needed |
| Search type | Full-text, within TypingMind chats | Full-text, across your existing chats, with jump-back |
| Where your data lives | Browser local storage | Local-first, on your device |
| Price | Paid license plus your API usage | Free |
| Best for | A unified custom chat interface | Searching the history you already have |

## What TypingMind does well

TypingMind is a capable power-user front end for people who want one polished interface over many models:

- **One interface, many models:** connect GPT, Claude, Gemini, and others through your own API keys.
- **Full-text chat search:** find past conversations inside TypingMind quickly, without endless scrolling.
- **Organisation:** folders, a prompt library, and plugins or integrations.
- **Local storage:** your TypingMind conversations are stored in your browser rather than on a vendor account.
- **No per-message markup from the app:** you pay model providers directly via API keys, which can be economical for heavy users.

For someone who wants to standardise on a single, customisable chat tool and is comfortable managing API keys, TypingMind is a strong choice.

## Where it falls short for retrieval

TypingMind's search is excellent, but only over the conversations it actually holds, and that is the catch.

- **It does not see your existing history.** Every chat you had in the official ChatGPT app, at claude.ai, or in the Gemini app is invisible to TypingMind. Its search covers TypingMind's own conversations, not your AI history at large.
- **It asks you to switch tools.** To get searchable history, you have to do your chatting inside TypingMind going forward, and route everything through API keys.
- **It is paid, with API costs.** There is a license to buy and model usage to fund. That is reasonable for the product, but it is a different proposition from a free add-on.

If your conversations already live in the official apps, a separate client cannot retroactively make that history searchable.

## A concrete example

You spent last month using the official ChatGPT and Claude apps for a project, and now you need the thread where you settled on a particular architecture. TypingMind cannot help: none of those conversations happened inside it. LLMnesia indexed them as you worked, so you search `event-driven vs polling tradeoff` and the exact ChatGPT and Claude messages surface with links back to the live chats. ## Can you use both together?

Yes. If you genuinely prefer TypingMind as your chat interface, keep it, and let LLMnesia index any conversations you still have on the official web apps. You then get TypingMind's in-client search for what you do there, plus LLMnesia's search for everything you do on ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, and the rest. ## Choosing between them

**Use TypingMind if:**
- You want a single custom chat interface over many models and are happy to switch your chatting into it.
- You are comfortable managing API keys and paying model providers directly.
- You want folders, a prompt library, and plugins in your chat tool.

**Use LLMnesia if:**
- You want to keep using the official ChatGPT, Claude, and Gemini apps.
- Your problem is searching the history you already have, not adopting a new client.
- You want a free, local-first option with no API keys to manage.

If your AI history already lives in the apps you use every day, the fastest path to searching it is to add a layer, not switch tools.
