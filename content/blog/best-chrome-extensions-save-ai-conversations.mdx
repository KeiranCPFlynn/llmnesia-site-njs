## Why native AI history is not enough

ChatGPT, Claude and Gemini offer conversation history. Finding a particular answer can still become difficult as your archive grows or your work spreads across platforms.

The problem is **search quality at volume**. Native history is designed for in-product browsing: a list of recent chats, a basic search bar, and titles you may or may not have set. This works well up to a point. Once you have hundreds of sessions across multiple platforms, recovering a specific prompt from three weeks ago takes more time than it should.

History-search extensions can add capture and retrieval across sites. Exporters can instead produce readable files for backup or reuse. Both can be useful. This roundup is published by LLMnesia; the additions below use vendor documentation checked on 18 October 2026 and are not a hands-on benchmark.

## What to look for in an AI conversation extension

Choose the job first: saving a readable file, organising chats, searching an archive or supplying context to an assistant. Different extensions can handle different parts of that workflow. Check:

- **Capture or export model:** decide whether you want ongoing capture or a file created on demand.
- **Archive search:** if retrieval is your goal, check whether search covers message content and multiple conversations.
- **Cross-platform support**: if you use more than one AI tool, coverage should match.
- **Local-first or clear privacy policy**: AI conversations often contain sensitive work. Know where your data goes.

## LLMnesia: search-first history for multi-platform users

LLMnesia is built around the retrieval problem. It runs in the background, indexes conversations from ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, and other supported platforms, and gives you a unified search interface for prompts, answers and code snippets in the captured archive.

The key differentiator is that LLMnesia is designed around **finding**, not just saving. Jump-back links return you directly to the original conversation. The index stays on your device.

Its [free local MCP connection](/mcp) also lets compatible desktop assistants consult captured conversations. Optional [Vault](/vault) adds paid end-to-end encrypted sync and backup. Retrieved content becomes context under the connected assistant's privacy terms.

Best for: users who switch between AI platforms and regularly need to recover context from captured sessions.

## AI Toolbox: search, folders and exports across four AI sites

[AI Toolbox](https://www.ai-toolbox.co/) combines cross-platform search with folders, bookmarks and reusable prompts for ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini and Grok. Its free tier returns five search results per query and includes unlimited single-chat TXT exports. Premium adds more formats and broader access.

Best for: people who want organisation and exports inside their AI websites. See the [AI Toolbox comparison](/compare/llmnesia-vs-ai-toolbox) for the search workflow and privacy distinctions.

## MemoryPlugin: shared memory and full chat-history retrieval

[MemoryPlugin](https://www.memoryplugin.com/) offers selected memories, opt-in full chat-history import and retrieval through browser and desktop integrations. Its service includes MCP access; it is not limited to summaries. It uses hosted storage and search, with paid plans and a trial.

Best for: people who want context supplied across supported tools and accept the service's cloud processing. Read [LLMnesia vs MemoryPlugin](/compare/llmnesia-vs-memoryplugin), including the distinction between encryption at rest and end-to-end encryption.

## Export tools: readable files for backup and handoffs

An exporter can be sufficient when you only need a few transcripts. [ChatGPT Exporter](https://www.chatgptexporter.com/en) saves ChatGPT chats; [AI Chat Exporter for Claude](https://www.ai-chat-exporter.net/en) serves Claude. Both vendors distinguish browser-generated formats from temporary server processing for PDFs.

[YourAIScroll](https://chromewebstore.google.com/detail/youraiscroll-ai-chat-hist/nhipdhjmedoeicanabmcmihlncjcanfe) advertises multi-platform export, including Markdown, PDF, DOCX, JSON, HTML and text. [Save my Chatbot](https://save.hugocollin.com/) lists thread downloads for Claude, Perplexity, Phind and ChatGPT. Check the exact listing and test a representative conversation before depending on complex content.

Best for: a readable document or a small handoff to another assistant. Exported files can be searched or uploaded as context where supported. The [portability guide](/blog/how-to-move-ai-chat-history-between-assistants) explains when files are enough and when retrieval helps.

## Chat Memo: simple auto-save for single-platform users

Chat Memo auto-saves conversations as you work. It is a lightweight option that adds a local backup layer without complexity.

The limitation is retrieval depth. Chat Memo is better suited for users who mainly need a backup copy rather than frequent keyword search across hundreds of sessions.

Best for: users who want a simple save solution and work primarily inside one platform.

## Echoes: established paid suite with AI summaries

Echoes (by R2bits) is one of the more established tools here, with around 10,000 users and search across 7 platforms: ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, Perplexity, AI Studio, DeepSeek, and Grok. Alongside fuzzy and boolean search it adds labels, bulk export, and an AI assistant that can summarise across your history.

The trade-offs are worth knowing. The AI assistant is capped at 50 queries per month on the free tier, and its best features sit behind a paid plan that adds an account and cross-device sync through Echoes' servers. Your conversation content stays in the browser, but the paid sync layer routes metadata off-device.

Best for: users who want a feature-rich paid suite and don't mind signing in. See the full [LLMnesia vs Echoes comparison](/compare/llmnesia-vs-echoes).

## Chat Lens: broad coverage with a prompt library

Chat Lens is a local-first, search-first extension that claims the widest coverage on this list, 18+ AI platforms, and bundles a prompt library, keyword bookmarks, and beta duplicate-question detection. It is a close peer to LLMnesia; the difference is feature breadth versus a lean, automatic retrieval focus.

Best for: users who want maximum platform coverage plus a prompt library. See the full [LLMnesia vs Chat Lens comparison](/compare/llmnesia-vs-chat-lens).

## ChatEngram: local-first with Obsidian and Notion export

ChatEngram auto-captures chats across ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, DeepSeek, and Perplexity in real time, stores them in your browser's local database, and offers global search via Cmd/Ctrl+K. Its standout is export: one-click Markdown to Obsidian, Notion, and Google Drive. It is newer and smaller than the tools above, but the export angle suits note-takers who want their AI history inside an existing knowledge base.

Best for: Obsidian or Notion users who want their AI chats exported into their notes.

## Personal AI Memory: open-source and fully local

Personal AI Memory silently auto-captures conversations from Claude, ChatGPT, Gemini, Perplexity, and Grok into local IndexedDB storage, with full-text search and nothing leaving your device. It is free, open-source with code on GitHub, and deliberately minimal. It is early and small, but a reasonable pick if auditability and a no-frills local approach matter to you.

Best for: privacy-minded users who want a free, open-source, fully local option.

## ChatHub: multi-model comparison, not history

ChatHub is often mentioned alongside history extensions but serves a different purpose entirely. It lets you prompt ChatGPT, Claude, and Gemini simultaneously in a side-by-side view.

ChatHub has no built-in conversation history indexing. It is the right tool for real-time model comparison, not for recovering past sessions.

Best for: teams benchmarking models or comparing responses to the same prompt.

## Promptly: prompt templates, not conversation history

Promptly manages reusable prompt templates. If your workflow requires standardized prompts across a team, it is worth evaluating. It does not address the problem of lost AI answers.

Best for: teams that need a shared prompt library, not users looking to search past conversations.

## Choose for your next task

A few readable exports may be enough for a one-off backup or project handoff. A growing archive benefits from search, whether that search lives in your note app, a history extension or a connected retrieval service.

Choose the simplest workflow that preserves what matters and lets you verify the answer. Keep the original transcripts even when you also save concise memories.
