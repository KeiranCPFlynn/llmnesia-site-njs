**Character.AI does not have a per-conversation export button — instead, you request a full export of your account data, which includes every chat, from your settings.** On web that's Profile Settings → Account → Manage Account & Data → export data. On mobile it's Export my data in your app settings. Either way, it's a request-and-wait process, not an instant download.

## Why this looks different from ChatGPT or Copilot

Platforms like ChatGPT and Microsoft Copilot let you export or save individual conversations one at a time — copy a response, print to PDF, or download a single chat. Character.AI's export works at the account level instead: one request pulls your whole data set, chats included, rather than a single storyline you pick out.

That makes it a good fit for a full backup, but a poor fit for grabbing one specific roleplay you want to keep. If it's a single thread you care about, copying the transcript or printing that conversation to PDF directly from the browser is faster than waiting on an account-wide export.

## How to export on web

1. Open Character.AI and sign in.
2. Go to **Profile Settings**.
3. Open **Account**, then **Manage Account & Data**.
4. Choose the **export data** option to request your account data, including conversation history.

Character.AI processes the request and makes a file available once it's ready. Because the exact timing and file format can change, the [Character.AI Help Centre article](https://support.character.ai/hc/en-us/articles/30299431702555-How-do-I-export-all-my-data-How-can-I-export-my-chats) is the source to check for the current specifics at the time you request it.

## How to export on mobile

In the Character.AI app, open your account settings and look for **Export my data**. It triggers the same account-wide export as the web version — there is no separate mobile-only export format.

## Saving a single conversation instead

If you only want one specific chat rather than your whole account, an account-wide export is overkill. For a single storyline you want to keep:

- Open the conversation and use your browser's print function (Ctrl+P or Cmd+P), then save as PDF.
- Or manually copy the transcript into a document if you want plain, editable text rather than a formatted PDF.

Either is faster than a full data request when it's just one thread you care about.

## Which method should you use?

The right method depends on whether you want everything or one thing, and whether you need to read it or edit it.

| You want... | Best method | Trade-off |
|---|---|---|
| A full backup of everything | Account data export | Request-and-wait; the file is a raw archive, not easy to browse |
| One specific chat, kept as-is | Print to PDF (Ctrl+P) | Visual snapshot; not editable text |
| One specific chat, as plain text | Copy the transcript | Manual; you paste into your own document |
| To keep finding chats day to day | A local search index | Set up once, then automatic (see below) |

For most people the account export is the "just in case" safety net, while print-to-PDF or copy-paste handles the handful of threads they actively want to keep. The one thing none of them do well is ongoing retrieval.

## What an export does not solve

An export is a snapshot of the moment you requested it, and the file itself is built for portability, not for reading. Opening it means digging through a raw archive of your account data, then using your text editor's find function to locate anything specific. It does not help you search or retrieve conversations day to day, and it does not update itself as you keep chatting, so every time your history moves on you would have to request a fresh one. If what you actually want is to find things inside your ongoing Character.AI history without repeating that whole process, that's a different problem, covered next.

It captures your active chat with each character — the one that opens from your recent chats — so if you run several parallel storylines with the same character, the export above is still the way to reach the older ones; LLMnesia's strength is making your ongoing history instantly searchable, not replacing a full account export.
