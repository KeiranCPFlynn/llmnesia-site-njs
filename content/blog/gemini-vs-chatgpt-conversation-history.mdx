Gemini and ChatGPT are the two highest-traffic consumer AI assistants, and many people use both. Both save your conversations, but they store them in different places, retain them on different rules, and give you different controls. If your question is "which one is better for finding what I discussed before," the honest answer is that ChatGPT has the more developed in-product history surface, while Gemini gives you more built-in retention control, and neither offers real full-text search of your conversation content.

This guide compares them directly on every dimension that matters for retrieval.

## The shared limitation

Start with what they have in common, because it shapes everything else: **neither platform lets you search the content of your past conversations well.** ChatGPT's native search matches conversation titles. Gemini lets you browse recent chats and manage your activity, but does not offer deep full-text search of message bodies. On both, a conversation about a specific topic will not surface unless you remember its title or roughly when it happened. That gap is the core retrieval problem on either platform.

## Where each platform stores history

**ChatGPT** stores conversation history on OpenAI's servers, tied to your account. Sign in on any device and the same history loads; nothing durable is kept on your device. For the full picture, see [where is ChatGPT history stored](/blog/where-is-chatgpt-history-stored).

**Gemini** stores history in your Google Account through the Gemini Apps Activity setting (also called Keep Activity). When it is on, your conversations are saved to your account and managed from your Gemini Apps Activity page. When it is off, conversations are still kept for up to 72 hours so Google can provide the service, but they are not added to your activity history.

## Retention and deletion controls

This is where the two genuinely differ.

| | Gemini | ChatGPT |
|---|---|---|
| Where history lives | Google Account (Gemini Apps Activity) | OpenAI servers, account-bound |
| Default retention | Auto-deletes activity older than 18 months | Kept until you delete it |
| Retention options | Change to 3 or 36 months, or turn auto-delete off | No built-in auto-delete schedule |
| When history is off | Kept up to 72 hours for service | Temporary Chat option available |
| Deleted-data removal | Managed via your activity controls | Removed from systems within about 30 days (standard practice) |

Gemini's model is built around Google's activity controls, so retention is configurable out of the box. ChatGPT keeps everything by default until you delete it, then removes deleted chats from its systems within roughly 30 days under standard practice. If granular auto-delete matters to you, Gemini gives you more dials; if "keep everything until I say otherwise" suits you, ChatGPT does that by default.

## Search and organisation

**ChatGPT** offers title-based search, and full-history search for Plus and Pro users, plus Projects (folders with their own instructions) on paid plans. Its in-product history is the more mature of the two for navigating a large archive.

**Gemini** surfaces recent conversations and integrates with Google's broader activity tooling, and offers Gems (custom assistants), but its conversation history is lighter on in-product search and organisation than ChatGPT's paid features.

For finding a specific old Gemini conversation, see [how to search Gemini conversation history](/blog/search-gemini-conversation-history). For the same on the ChatGPT side, the methods mirror those in [ChatGPT vs Claude conversation history](/blog/chatgpt-vs-claude-conversation-history).

## Export

Both platforms let you get your data out. ChatGPT offers a data export from Settings that includes conversations as JSON and human-readable HTML. Gemini history can be exported through Google's data tools (Google Takeout and the activity controls). Exports are point-in-time snapshots: useful for backup, but you then have to search the files yourself.

## The multi-platform reality

If you use only one of these, you live with that platform's limits. If you use both, the problem compounds: two separate histories, in two separate accounts, with no shared search. The conversation where Gemini drafted an outline and the follow-up where ChatGPT refined it sit in different places, and neither platform can see the other.

[LLMnesia](https://llmnesia.com/) is a free, local-first Chrome extension that indexes your conversations from both Gemini and ChatGPT (plus Claude, Perplexity, and 10+ platforms) as you browse them, and makes them full-text searchable from one place.

- **Full-text search** across the content of every indexed conversation, not just titles.
- **Cross-platform:** one query returns Gemini and ChatGPT results together, with jump-back links.
- **Local-first:** the index is stored on your device, independent of either platform's retention rules.

[Install LLMnesia from the Chrome Web Store](https://chromewebstore.google.com/detail/llmnesia/leekfgbdojiaabifbjbbgiiclannjdkf) to search both histories at once.

## Which should you choose?

For most people the choice between Gemini and ChatGPT is about answer quality, ecosystem, and price, not history features, because both fall short on content search. If history specifically drives your decision:

- **Choose Gemini** if you want built-in, configurable retention and tight integration with your Google Account's activity controls.
- **Choose ChatGPT** if you want the more developed in-product history surface, with full-history search and Projects on paid plans.
- **Use both** and you will feel the cross-platform search gap quickly. That is the case where a local index across both pays off most.
