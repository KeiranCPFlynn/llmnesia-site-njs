You can delete all your ChatGPT conversations in a few clicks from Data Controls, or remove them one at a time from the sidebar. Bulk deletion is permanent through the interface, so the important step is keeping anything you might need before you clear everything. This post covers how to delete all chats, how to remove individual ones, what actually happens to the data, and how to preserve what matters first.

## Three ways to remove conversations

Pick the method that matches what you want to achieve.

| Goal | Method | Reversible? |
|---|---|---|
| Wipe everything | Clear or delete all in Data Controls | No |
| Remove a few chats | Delete each from the sidebar | No |
| Declutter without losing | Archive instead of delete | Yes |

## How to delete all conversations at once

1. Open ChatGPT and go to **Settings**.
2. Select **Data Controls**.
3. Use the option to clear or delete all conversations.
4. Confirm when prompted.

This removes every conversation from your account in a single action. Because there is no user-facing trash to restore from, treat it as permanent. If you are clearing history to start fresh rather than to remove something specific, consider archiving first so you keep a fallback.

## How to delete individual conversations

If you only want to remove certain chats:

1. Open the conversation list in the sidebar.
2. Open the menu for the specific conversation.
3. Choose delete and confirm.

This is the right approach when most of your history is worth keeping and only a few chats need to go.

## Archive instead, if you only want a cleaner list

A common mistake is deleting conversations to tidy the sidebar, then realising later you needed one. Archiving solves the clutter without the risk: it removes a conversation from the main list but keeps it in your account, and you can restore it from the archived chats area in settings. If your goal is organisation rather than removal, archive.

## What actually happens to deleted data

Deleting removes conversations from your account and they stop appearing in your history. OpenAI does not offer a way to restore deleted chats through the interface, so from your perspective they are gone. Providers may retain limited data for a short period for legal and abuse-prevention reasons, which is separate from your access to it. For the detail on this, see [does ChatGPT keep deleted conversations?](/blog/does-chatgpt-keep-deleted-conversations).

One important distinction: deletion is not the tool for controlling training. Turning off model training in Data Controls is forward-looking and does not retroactively remove data already used. Deleting a conversation removes it from your account but is a different action with a different purpose.

## Keep what matters before you clear everything

The risk with a full history wipe is deleting something you later need: a useful answer, a piece of code, a draft. Two ways to protect yourself first:

- **Export your data.** ChatGPT can produce a download of your conversations. Good as a one-time archive. See [how to back up AI conversations](/blog/how-to-backup-ai-conversations).
- **Keep a local index.** LLMnesia is a free, local-first Chrome extension that searches your AI chat history across ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini and 10+ platforms. It indexes your conversations on your own device as you browse them, so even after you delete everything from ChatGPT, you retain a searchable local copy of what was said. Index first, then delete with confidence.

If you also want to stop saving new conversations going forward, see [how to use ChatGPT without saving history](/blog/how-to-use-chatgpt-without-saving-history).

## Quick recommendation

1. Decide whether you want removal (delete) or just a cleaner list (archive).
2. Before a full wipe, export or index your conversations so you keep what is useful.
3. Use Delete all in Data Controls for everything, or per-chat delete for a few.
4. Remember deletion is permanent through the interface, and separate from training controls.
