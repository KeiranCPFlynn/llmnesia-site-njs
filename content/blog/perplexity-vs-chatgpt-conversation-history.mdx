Perplexity and ChatGPT both save your conversations, but they organise and surface them differently, and neither offers strong native full-text search of message content. ChatGPT keeps a sidebar list of chats; Perplexity is built around threads grouped into Spaces and a Library. If you use one or both for serious work, the practical question is how to find a specific answer weeks later. This guide compares them directly on retrieval.

## Perplexity vs ChatGPT at a glance

| Dimension | Perplexity | ChatGPT |
|---|---|---|
| History model | Threads, Spaces, Library | Sidebar conversation list |
| Organisation | Group threads into Spaces/Library | Projects on paid plans |
| Search type | Title and collection oriented | Title-based |
| Search inside a chat | Browser Ctrl+F in an open thread | Browser Ctrl+F in an open chat |
| Strength | Sourced research, grouping threads | Drafting, reasoning, broad ecosystem |
| Cross-platform search | No (Perplexity only) | No (ChatGPT only) |

The headline: they are designed for different jobs, and both share the same retrieval weakness once your archive grows.

## How each handles history

**Perplexity** is research-oriented. Conversations are threads, and you can organise related threads into Spaces or your Library. That structure is genuinely useful for keeping a research project together, and it is more collection-minded than ChatGPT's flat list. Where it is weak is full-text retrieval: finding a specific sentence or fact buried inside one of many threads relies on you remembering which thread and which Space it is in. See [Perplexity Spaces vs conversation history](/blog/perplexity-spaces-vs-conversation-history).

**ChatGPT** keeps a sidebar list of conversations, with Projects available on paid plans to group related chats. Its native search matches conversation titles rather than the content of messages, so an answer inside a long chat with a vague title is hard to surface.

## The shared limitation: finding content, not titles

Both platforms let you browse and organise, and both fall short at the same thing: searching the actual words inside your past conversations. Titles are auto-generated and vague; Spaces and Projects help grouping but do not give you full-text search across everything. The substance you remember, a statistic, a phrasing, a snippet, lives in the message body, which native search does not reach.

This compounds with use. After months of research threads and chats, the chance that a specific answer is findable by title or by remembering the right Space drops sharply.

## Worked example: where did that source go?

Say last month you used Perplexity to research three competitors and saved the threads to a Space, and separately used ChatGPT to draft a summary. Today you need the exact figure one source gave. With native tools you would open the right Perplexity Space, guess which thread, and scan it, then check whether the figure also made it into the ChatGPT draft. Two interfaces, two manual hunts, neither searching content directly.

## Which should you choose?

- Choose **Perplexity** when your work is sourced research and you want to group threads into Spaces.
- Choose **ChatGPT** for drafting, reasoning, and its broader ecosystem.
- For finding past answers in either, add a full-text, cross-platform search layer, because neither solves content retrieval on its own.
