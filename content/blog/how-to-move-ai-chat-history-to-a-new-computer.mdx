Getting a new computer does not have to mean losing access to your AI conversations. For the major platforms, your chat history is stored against your account on their servers, so signing into the same account on the new machine restores the same history. The part that does not transfer automatically is anything stored on the device itself, such as a local search index. This post explains what moves on its own, what you need to bring across deliberately, and how to keep a portable, searchable copy.

## What transfers automatically, and what does not

| Item | Moves to a new computer by itself? | What to do |
|---|---|---|
| ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini history | Yes, it is tied to your account | Sign in with the same account |
| Memory and custom instructions | Yes, account-based | Confirm they appear after sign-in |
| A data export file | Only if you copy the file across | Copy it to the new machine |
| A local-first extension's index | No, it lives on the old device | Reinstall and re-index |
| Browser bookmarks to chats | Only via browser sync | Enable browser sync or re-save |

The simple rule: account-based data follows your login; device-based data does not. Most people are surprised only by the last two rows.

## Step 1: Restore account-based history

For ChatGPT, Claude, and Gemini, your conversations live on the platform's servers, attached to your account, not to your old computer. To get them back:

1. Install your browser on the new computer.
2. Sign in to each AI platform with the exact same account you used before, including the right sign-in method (Google, email, work account).
3. Confirm your conversation list, memory, and custom instructions appear.

If history looks missing, you are almost always on a different account or sign-in method. For where this data actually lives, see [where is ChatGPT history stored?](/blog/where-is-chatgpt-history-stored).

## Step 2: Export before you switch, as a safety net

Account-based history is convenient but it is not a backup you control. Before retiring the old machine, export your conversations from each platform you rely on. Exports give you a file you own, independent of the account, which protects you if a login is lost or a chat is later deleted. Walk through it in [how to back up AI conversations](/blog/how-to-backup-ai-conversations).

Copy the export files to the new computer (or cloud storage) along with everything else you are migrating.

## Step 3: Reinstall your search and indexing tools

This is the step people miss. If you use a local-first tool that indexes your conversations on the device, that index lives on the old computer and does not ride along with your account. It is a privacy feature, not a bug: the data was never on someone else's server to sync.

On the new machine, reinstall the tool and let it re-index. Because the conversations themselves come back when you sign into your accounts, the tool simply rebuilds its searchable index from the chats as you browse them.

## A migration checklist

Before and after switching computers, run through this:

1. Export conversations from each platform you use.
2. Copy export files to the new machine or cloud storage.
3. Sign into each AI platform with the same account on the new computer.
4. Confirm history, memory, and custom instructions appear.
5. Reinstall your search extension and let it re-index.
6. Keep the old machine's data until you have verified everything on the new one.

Follow that order and a new computer means a fresh start for the hardware, not for your AI history.
