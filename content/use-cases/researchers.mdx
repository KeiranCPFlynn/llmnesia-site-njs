Use LLMnesia to revisit a saved AI discussion before you repeat an analysis or change a research method. Find the conversation, recover the assumptions behind the decision, then verify the original sources before carrying the conclusion into your work.

The result is a short decision record linked to the discussion and your research notes. Finding an AI answer does not verify it, and a searchable conversation is not a substitute for a paper, dataset or citation manager.

## Prepare the history you want to search

Install LLMnesia in Chrome or Edge on a computer. Open the supported AI websites you used for the project and use the LLMnesia panel's Import option to save available older conversations. Check each import result before relying on the coverage.

Search covers the conversations LLMnesia has saved or imported. It cannot reconstruct deleted discussions, unavailable attachments or messages the provider does not return. Keep your working papers, datasets and analysis files in their normal research storage.

## A worked example: why did we reject this method?

Suppose you are revisiting a survey analysis called **River Study**. You remember discussing missing responses with Claude and exploring alternatives in ChatGPT. The names and queries here are illustrative, not records added to your index.

| What you need to recover | Search to try | What to inspect |
| --- | --- | --- |
| The project discussion | `River Study` | Is this the relevant survey and analysis stage? |
| An assumption | `missing responses` | Was the assumption checked, or only proposed? |
| An alternative method | The method's name | Why was it considered, changed or rejected? |
| A cited paper | An author name or title fragment | Does the original source exist and support the claim? |

Start with a distinctive term, then refine the query. If the discussion was split across several chats, open each relevant result and keep its context separate. Similar wording does not establish that two chats concern the same dataset.

## Separate suggestions from decisions

Read around the matching message and look for:

1. **The inputs:** which question, dataset version and constraints were supplied?
2. **The proposal:** what did the assistant suggest, and what assumptions did it make?
3. **The decision:** what did you or your collaborators actually choose, and why?
4. **The limits:** what remained uncertain, untested or dependent on missing information?

Do not promote an exploratory AI suggestion into a research decision because it appeared in a search result. The final choice may be recorded in a lab note or analysis file outside the conversation. Check that record too.

## Follow the source trail back to the evidence

For any claim you plan to reuse, open the original paper, dataset or official documentation. Check the author, title and date, then locate the passage or result that supports the claim. A source link in an AI answer is a starting point for this check, not the check itself.

Keep the verified source in your citation manager. Add the relevant page, section or data version to your research notes. If you cannot verify a citation or recover the underlying source, mark the claim as unverified rather than carrying it into a draft as evidence.

Use the [academic citation guide](/blog/how-to-cite-ai-conversations-academic) when you also need to disclose or cite AI assistance. Follow the requirements of your institution, journal or project.

## Save a decision record for the next session

Write a compact note in your existing project records:

```text
Project and question: River Study, missing survey responses
Analysis/data version: [version or file reference]
Decision: [what the researcher chose]
Reason: [assumptions and checks supporting that choice]
Alternatives rejected: [option and reason]
Source discussion: [matching conversation link]
Verified evidence: [paper, dataset, page or section]
Open questions: [what still needs checking]
```

This is a manual record you assemble from the evidence. LLMnesia finds the discussion; it does not automatically determine which decision was approved or which source is correct.

If the saved chat is incomplete, record that limitation. Do not fill a missing reasoning step with a plausible reconstruction and present it as the original decision.

## Fit the workflow into your research tools

Use your citation manager for literature and bibliographic records, your analysis environment for reproducible work, and LLMnesia to find saved AI conversations that help explain how you got there. Link the records when useful instead of treating any one tool as the complete project archive.

For broader organisation habits, read [AI chat history for researchers](/blog/ai-chat-history-for-researchers). This page focuses on the smaller task of recovering and checking one decision before you continue working.

## Where your saved copy lives

The free extension keeps its search index on your device. [Vault](/vault) optionally adds encrypted backup and sync. Neither changes the original AI provider's handling of your inputs or replaces your normal research storage and backups.
