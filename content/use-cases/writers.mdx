Use LLMnesia to find a saved draft and the conversation that shaped it, then take a short revision brief back to your editor. This workflow is useful when you remember a line that worked but cannot remember which AI tool, chat, or prompt produced it.

LLMnesia searches conversations it has already saved or imported. It does not choose your final draft, compare versions for you, or restore writing that the original service no longer makes available.

## Start with the writing history you can actually search

Install LLMnesia in Chrome or Edge on a computer. Open each supported AI website where you worked, then use the LLMnesia panel's Import option to bring in available older conversations. Check the import result before assuming all of your drafts are searchable.

If a search finds nothing, check whether that platform's history has been imported. Also try a different phrase or the original website's history. An empty result does not establish that a draft was deleted.

## A worked example: recover the opening that worked

Imagine you are revising a client newsletter called **Harbour Notes**. You tried several openings in Claude and ChatGPT. You remember the line *the repair starts before the replacement*, but not the conversation title. The names and phrases below are examples, not sample content added to your index.

| What you remember | Search to try | What to check in the result |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A distinctive line | `repair starts` | Does the matching message contain the opening you meant? |
| The publication name | `Harbour Notes` | Is this the right issue and audience? |
| A voice instruction | `short sentences` | Did that instruction apply to this draft or another project? |
| A rejected angle | `replacement` | Did you reject this version later in the conversation? |

Start with one distinctive term or a short query. Refine it after seeing the results. If you use several words, check the search matching option rather than assuming the typed word `AND` is an operator.

## Read the decisions around the draft

Open a matching conversation and check the messages before and after the draft. Recover three things:

1. **The brief:** audience, purpose, length and constraints you supplied.
2. **The revision:** the changes you requested after the first answer.
3. **The decision:** any message explaining why you kept or rejected that version.

Keep the conversation link with your working notes. If the original chat is unavailable, use the saved text LLMnesia has, with its limits in mind. A saved copy may not include attachments or messages the provider did not return.

## Turn the result into a revision brief

Copy the passages you need into your normal document or editor. Keep the approved draft there, with a small brief for the next session:

```text
Piece: Harbour Notes, November issue
Working draft: [link to the document you are editing]
Source conversation: [link from the matching result]
Keep: the opening about repairing before replacing
Change: shorten the middle section and add the verified example
Voice: short sentences; avoid sales language
Do not repeat: the rejected price-led opening
```

This brief is something you write from the conversation. LLMnesia does not generate it automatically. Review it before pasting it into another AI service, especially when the source contains client information.

## Compare versions in your editor

Find another relevant conversation when you need a second version. Put the two passages side by side in your editor and label where each came from. Check meaning, factual claims, voice and your client's changes before selecting one.

Do not treat the newest conversation as the final approval. Approval may live in an email or document outside the AI chat. Your working document should remain the place where you record the version you chose.

## Where your saved copy lives

The free extension's search index stays on your device. [Vault](/vault) is the optional encrypted backup and sync service. Neither changes the data handling of an AI service you already use, and neither replaces your normal document backups.
